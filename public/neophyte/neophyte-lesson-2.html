01. Salience & Inflation

Salience is the "voltage" of a symbol—how important it feels to you. When you open a Tarot deck, you are deliberately increasing salience. You are telling your brain: "Look here. This matters."

The danger is Inflation. This occurs when the pattern-matching engine of the brain overheats, finding connections where there are none, leading to paranoia, obsession, or "Main Character Syndrome."

We prevent this not by avoiding the cards, but by installing Circuit Breakers (Silence and Stop Rules).

The Jungian Warning

Carl Jung, who spent decades working with mandalas, alchemical imagery, and archetypal symbolism, wrote extensively about what he called psychic inflation. In his autobiography Memories, Dreams, Reflections, he describes his own encounter with overwhelming archetypal forces during his confrontation with the unconscious (1913-1919). He writes:

"The images of the unconscious place a great responsibility upon a man. Failure to understand them, or a shirking of ethical responsibility, deprives him of his wholeness and imposes a painful fragmentariness on his life."

— C.G. Jung, Memories, Dreams, Reflections (1962)

Jung's warning is clear: archetypal work is dangerous without structure. The Tarot, like active imagination or alchemical meditation, opens a channel to the autonomous complexes of the psyche. If the ego identifies with these forces rather than relating to them, inflation occurs. You begin to believe you are the Magician, rather than observing the Magician as a pattern.

The Mechanism of Inflation

When you draw a card, your brain's pattern-recognition system activates. This is the same system that helps you recognize faces, predict social dynamics, and navigate complex environments. It operates largely below conscious awareness, and it's incredibly good at finding patterns—sometimes too good.

The problem is that high salience + confirmation bias = apophenia (seeing patterns where none exist). The more emotionally invested you become in the reading, the more your brain will find "evidence" that confirms your interpretation. This is not mysticism; this is neuroscience.
